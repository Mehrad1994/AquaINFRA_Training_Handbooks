## Key points

- **The hook:** scuba divers at the Latvian Institute of Aquatic Ecology noticed the water getting "darker" over the years - a throwaway remark that became a testable hypothesis.
- **The variable: [Secchi depth]({{ relative_root }}reference/glossary#secchi-depth).** A black-and-white disc is lowered until it disappears; that depth is a proxy for transparency, and hence for algae, particles, and organic matter. The Baltic has decades of these records.
- **Why this gulf:** shallow, semi-enclosed, and slow to exchange water with the open Baltic - so river runoff and land-based activity leave a strong, detectable signal.
- **Why season matters:** spring nutrient inputs, summer algal blooms, and winter ice each shift the optics - so the analysis is done *per season*, not just per year.
- **The name:** the workflow is called **DGA** after the **Daugava**, the largest river flowing into the gulf.

---

## ✅ Key takeaways

- A qualitative observation ("water looks darker") is tested with quantitative trend analysis.
- **Secchi depth** is the transparency proxy - simple, cheap, long historical record.
- The gulf's geography plus strong seasonality make it a natural lab for per-season trend detection.
