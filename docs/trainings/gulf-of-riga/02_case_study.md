## The origin story {#origin-story}

The research question began with a personal observation from scuba divers at the **Latvian Institute of Aquatic Ecology**: they jokingly noted that the underwater world in the Gulf of Riga seemed to be becoming "darker" over the years.

That throw-away comment turned out to be a testable hypothesis. **Secchi depth** - a long-running, simple optical measurement of how far you can see into the water - has been recorded across the Baltic for decades. If the divers were right, those records should show a downward trend.

## Why the Gulf of Riga

- **Semi-enclosed basin** - pollution and runoff signals are easier to detect than in open ocean.
- **Long Secchi-depth time series** - sufficient data to apply statistical trend tests.
- **Single large river input** (the Daugava) - makes catchment-driven hypotheses tractable.

---

## ✅ Key takeaways

- The case study tests a real, qualitative observation ("water is getting darker") with quantitative methods.
- **Secchi depth** is the variable of interest - simple, cheap, long-historical-record.
- The Gulf of Riga's geography makes it a natural lab for this kind of analysis.
